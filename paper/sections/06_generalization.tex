\section{Energy gaps at other orders}\label{sec:generalization}
Let $3\mid n$, $H\in\muT^{n\times n}$, $G=HH^*$, $K=H^*H$, and $Q=\sum_{i<j}\N(G_{ij})$. The two Gram energies agree. The row-color congruences of \cref{lem:colors} hold at every such order. We call $H$ a Butson matrix if $G=nI$.

\begin{lemma}[Restriction of an orthogonal frame]\label{lem:frame-new}
Suppose $Y^*Y=nI_r$, $|Y_{ij}|=1$, and $Y$ has $n$ rows. For any $d$ rows indexed by $A$,
\[dr=\|Y_A\|_F^2\le n\rank Y_A.\]
In particular, if those rows are linearly dependent then $n\le d(n-r)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every singular value of a row restriction is at most $\sqrt n$. Sum their squares; then use $\rank Y_A\le d-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pure-color energy]\label{thm:pure-general}
If both colorings are pure and $H$ is not Butson, then $Q\ge9\lceil n/2\rceil$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $G=nI+3T$, $K=nI+3S$, and $Q=9u$. Then $TH=HS$. If either support has no isolate, its edge count is at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$. Otherwise let the nonempty core orders be $v\ge v'$. The $n-v'$ isolated columns form an orthogonal frame. A minimum-degree vertex of $T$ has $d\le2u/v$ neighbors; its row equation against the isolated columns gives a nonzero dependence among those $d$ rows. Thus
\[n\le dv'\le 2uv'/v\le2u.\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The first gap and its equality case]\label{thm:first-gap}
Either $H$ is Butson, or $Q\ge3(n-1)$. Equality holds precisely when changing one entry of $H$ produces a Butson matrix. At equality,
\[|\det H|^2=n^{n-2}(n^2-3n+3).\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A mixed coloring has at least $n-1$ cross-color pairs; the pure case follows from \cref{thm:pure-general}. At equality both colorings are $(n-1,1)$, with orthogonal majority rows and columns. Put the minority row and column last and write
\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}A&x\\y^T&z\end{pmatrix},\quad
 G-nI=\begin{pmatrix}0&g\\g^*&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 K-nI=\begin{pmatrix}0&h\\h^*&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The upper-left block identity gives $gy^T=xh^*$, so $g=\alpha x$, $h^*=\alpha y^T$, $\N(\alpha)=3$. Since $g=A\bar y+x\bar z$, the upper-right block gives $\alpha z+\bar\alpha\bar z=3$. Thus $\alpha z\in\{1-\omega,1-\omega^2\}$. The entry $z'=z-\bar\alpha$ is a different third root, and replacing $z$ by $z'$ cancels every hub correlation. Conversely, a one-entry change in a Butson matrix creates exactly $n-1$ correlations of norm three. The star Gram has eigenvalues $n$ ($n-2$ times) and $n\pm\sqrt{3(n-1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The second mixed-color gap]\label{thm:second-gap}
Let $n\ge6$. If either coloring is mixed, then
\[Q=3(n-1)\quad\hbox{or}\quad Q\ge6(n-2).\]
If $BH(n,3)$ exists, both values are attained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $Q<6(n-2)$. Both colorings must be $(n-1,1)$ and
$Q=3(n-1)+9m$, with $m<(n-3)/3$. Normalize the minority row and column to ones. Let $A,B$ be the internal majority defects, with core orders $v,v'$, and ordinary sets $R,R'$. Both cores have at most $m$ edges, so $|R|,|R'|\ge n-1-2m>n/3$.
The $R\times R'$ block identity makes the ordinary hub correlations a common scalar $\alpha$. If $i$ is a leaf of $A$ with neighbor $a$, its equation reads
\[A_{ia}H_{a,R'}=(\alpha-g_i)\one^T.\]
If $s$ distinct vertices are neighbors of leaves, their rows together with the minority row restrict to rank one on $R'$. The frame bound gives $(s+1)|R'|\le n$, so $s\le1$. If a leaf existed, this would force $v'\ge n/2-1$. A graph with at most one leaf-neighbor vertex has at least $v'-1$ edges, whence $v'\le m+1<n/3$, a contradiction. Both cores are therefore leafless, with $v,v'\le m$.
If a core is nonempty, choose $v\ge v'$ and a minimum degree $2\le d\le2m/v$. Its equation gives a dependence among its $d$ neighbor rows and the minority row. Hence
\[n\le(d+1)(v'+1)\le(2m/v+1)(v+1)\le3m+3<n.\]
The penultimate inequality is $(v-2)(v-m)\le0$. Thus both cores are empty. Intertwining then makes all hub correlations have equal magnitude; their norm is $3\pmod9$ and the assumed energy range forces norm three. This is the first gap. For the second value, multiply two entries in one row of a Butson matrix by the same nontrivial third root; the column energy is $3\cdot2(n-2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Odd norm obstruction]\label{thm:odd-gap}
If $n^n$ is not an Eisenstein norm, a pure coloring on both sides forces $Q\ge9(n+3)/2$. Every third-root matrix at such an order satisfies
\[Q\ge\min\{9(n+3)/2,\,6(n-2)\}.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The order is odd and some $p\equiv2\pmod3$ has odd valuation in $n$. The pure bound leaves only $u=(n+1)/2$ to exclude. If a support is isolate-free, every edge has unit normalized norm and the support is $P_3\sqcup\frac{n-3}{2}K_2$. Its determinant is
\[n(n^2-18)(n^2-9)^{(n-3)/2},\]
which has odd $p$-valuation.
If both supports have isolates, the frame bound gives $nv\le(n+1)v'$ and its reverse, so $v=v'<n$. Minimum core degree is $d=\lceil n/v\rceil$; the degree sum is the even integer $n+1$, and all edges have norm one. Either $vd=n$, with one vertex of degree $d+1$, or $vd=n+1$ and the core is regular.
Let $Z$ be the isolated columns and $P=I-H_{:,Z}H_{:,Z}^*/n$. It is a projection of constant diagonal $v/n$. In the first case each degree-$d$ neighborhood supports a vector in $\operatorname{im}P$, and its principal block has trace one. Thus that block is rank one, has positive diagonal and is separated from its complement. Intersecting such neighborhoods coincide. Applying this to all neighbors of the unique degree-$(d+1)$ vertex contradicts degree $d$ at every other vertex.
In the regular case $\operatorname{im}T\subseteq\operatorname{im}P$, so
\[v/d\le\rank T\le v^2/n=v^2/(vd-1).\]
The lower bound follows from $\|T\|\le d$ and $\tr T^2=vd$. Write $v=ad+b$, $0\le b<d$. If $b>0$, an integer rank at least $a+1$ would require
\[0\ge(a+1)(vd-1)-v^2=v(d-b)-(a+1)>0.\]
Thus $d\mid v$ and the rank is $v/d$. Equality forces the nonzero eigenvalues to be $d,-d$ with equal multiplicities. Consequently
\[\det G=n^{n-v/d}(n^2-9d^2)^{v/(2d)}.\]
Since $v/d$ is even and $p\nmid d$, this again has odd $p$-valuation. The mixed case follows from \cref{thm:first-gap,thm:second-gap}, since the first equality layer would repair to a Butson matrix.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Determinant consequences and scope}
Define
\[V_n(q)=(n-t)^{n-1}(n+(n-1)t),\qquad t=\sqrt{2q/(n(n-1))}.\]
The envelope decreases with $q$, so each energy lower bound gives a determinant upper bound by substitution. At norm-obstructed orders $n\ge18$ the result is $|\det H|^2\le V_n(9(n+3)/2)$. These normalized bounds tend to one.
The Gram and frame arguments apply to arbitrary unit-modulus alphabets. Prime alphabets give the balanced-difference correspondence of \cref{prop:prime-code}; for composite alphabets all nontrivial characters are needed to characterize a difference matrix.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gap}
If a sequence of square unit-modulus matrices satisfies $Q_n\ge cn^2$ for fixed $c>0$, then
\[\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\det H_n|/n^{n/2}\le[(1+\sqrt{2c})e^{-\sqrt{2c}}]^{1/2}<1.\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $cn^2$ in the envelope; $t=\sqrt{2cn/(n-1)}\to\sqrt{2c}$.
\end{proof}
A quadratic energy bound in an infinite congruence class remains a separate problem.
